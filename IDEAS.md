# IDEAS — the idea registry

Ideas that are **not built**. An idea is built only when it passes the four
gates (`DESIGN.md` §2: serves a core law, opens or strengthens an underdog
path, readable on screen, reuses an existing model) and has an owner in the
coverage table (`DESIGN.md` §8). Moving an idea out of this file is a
decision: log it in `DECISIONS.md` and reflect it in `DESIGN.md`.

| The hull radiates, fed by cooling manifolds (from the owner's discussion, 2026-10-23) | **The problem it answers** (the kernel's own numbers, `js/battle.js` buildShip of R1 at T1, reactor demand 1, engine throttle 1, shield on, 1800 s of `step`, a scratch run, nothing recorded): with all 17 radiators the first room reaches hot at 1060 s, with none at 989 s; the panels radiate 0.15 / 2.8 / 8.9 MW at 290 / 600 / 800 K against about 25 MW made; the coolant loop (74.2 MJ/K) has a time constant of 28–223 min; a 30 s Cool at 600 K sheds about 1.1 K. So radiators barely matter in a 3–6 min battle, heat is a per-battle budget, and since L5b only panels and the exhaust radiate (an R1 with no radiators passes validateShip and is IR-dark when coasting). **The idea**: the radiator fitting becomes a **cooling manifold**: a room's open hull edges radiate at the room's temperature (a dark coating, emissivity about 0.9) while a live manifold on that room pumps coolant behind its skin; without one, only a weak passive leak. R1 has 38 open hull edges (950 m², twice its panels): 6.3 MW at 600 K, 19.9 MW at 800 K, so a hot ship nears balance at full burn (equilibrium about 850–960 K, by which edges join the loop), and losing manifolds while hot overheats it in minutes. **A cascade with the heat sink** (the owner's): the sink is 27 of the loop's 74.2 MJ/K; at 25 MW, +200 K takes 594 s intact, 378 s without the sink, 275 s without the sink and the shield generator; shooting the sink, then the manifolds, cooks the enemy. Gates claimed, not measured: throughput (heat a flow once hot); dependency (a small fitting, a large effect); information (the hull glows by the room behind it, a deep reactor stays dark, a section going dim is a tell); an underdog path (cascade: sink, manifolds, overheat; facing chooses which manifolds are exposed); readable (glowing hull sections); reuses the heat model (T⁴ per hull segment, as a panel radiates today). Alternatives weighed in the discussion: keep radiators as they are (heat a budget; #297's "strip radiators -> overheat" does not hold inside a battle); remove radiators (loses most of the IR layer of the information law); larger sci-fi panels (one constant beyond its range); venting coolant (water about 3 MJ/kg, hydrogen about 8.7 MJ/kg but 14 times the volume); a heat pump (about 17 MW of work to lift 25 MW from 600 to 1000 K, beyond a 20 MW reactor) | Replaces a proven model (radiator panels, L1–L2 scenarios move) and a §4 law's wording: the owner's call, the lead designer's model and cost. Only enriches play if battles reach plant hot: **the measure** is the share of L5e battles in which a side reaches plant hot, with and without the heat-sink cascade; if low, the idea only tidies the shipyard. One manifold per room may be too binary (redundancy: two). Unverified: whether the reactor's waste heat follows the power used or its demand setting (the run held demand at 1); the coating's emissivity; `thermal.plantOverheated` (650–950 K) against the equilibrium. Consequences found after (2026-10-23): (1) L5c's IR seeker homes on the **nearest** resolved panel or exhaust, not the hottest (`js/missile.js`), so a hotter ship is resolved from farther but not hit where it is hottest, and a burning engine's exhaust is always resolved; under this idea a room whose manifold is lost goes **dim** (its heat trapped inside) while the rooms still cooled glow; (2) the cascade ends at Disabled before the crew die: an overheated reactor shuts down, no weapon is able, the battle ends; after it the prize's life support stops (the bridge keeps its backup) and its crewed rooms and magazine heat through passive walls (a hand estimate: about 0.05 K/s from an 800 K neighbour over a 10 m wall, crew at 380 K in about 25–30 min, a magazine at 450 K in about 50 min): a campaign beat (restore the prize's cooling or lose its crew and perhaps the prize; #79: no bonus for sparing them); (3) life support pumps heat into the coolant loop at no cost (§17 item 14 defers its draw and heat): lifting heat from 295 K into an 850–960 K loop costs about twice the heat moved even for an ideal pump, so this idea makes item 14 matter | waiting |
