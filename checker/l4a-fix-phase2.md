## Discrepancies (first)

| # | What |
|---|---|
| D1 | **Cluster vs ship: a destroyed cell's heat share.** My phase 1 had round 3 of `round d 0.1 ×4` venting ¼ of the 4-cell hold with the plate (F5) and `capacity d 0.2` / the ×4 case leaving C = 3 × 1.35e6 after the mount cell is destroyed. The kernel gives **C = 5.4e6** (four cells' worth) in both: in a cluster a destroyed hold cell keeps its capacity and heat; only a heat sink (`ON_CELL_DESTROYED.heatSink`) and a *ship* (`rebalance`, `ventCell` on lost mass, `js/rooms.js:1381`) vent a cell's share (#219 Q6). The criterion's 'returns to 5.4e6' is consistent with the kernel but not with the ship rule for the same event. Not an error of this fix round (the plate's own share is right: vented 63 821.1 J = 17 662 500 × 3.6134e-3 K, matches mine), but a plate on a destroyed cell **in a ship** (L4b) will vent both, and no document says a cluster differs. Lead designer to write it into 13l.1 or 13d.1. |
| D2 | **The round's velocity with two hulls.** §4 and §13l.1 say `fireRound`/`shieldRound` take the round's velocity relative to the hull; #258 (11) says each share is booked on its own generator's body. With bodies moving differently, one round has one world velocity, yet the scenario (and the kernel) take u_i = hull_i at the round's point + v for each share. The sum of the generators' heat is then not the world-frame energy loss of a single round (differences ≈ m s_i Δv (hull_i − w) ~ 1e-4 of the heat for B's 0.036 m/s). It is a coherent per-generator Galilean rule (heat_i independent of the body's V), and my hand check agrees with it exactly (below), but the documents do not state it; the criterion does. Also the rotational part L²/2I is included in dK_i (my F7 guessed J²/2M only: A's gain is 28.78 J, of which 13.43 J is spin). Both belong in 13l.1's 'Rounds in overlapping fields' row. |
| D3 | **`ruin d 0.2` is on a threshold, resolved by a snap.** 1/burn = 1413.0000000000002: in plain float the plate is still positive after step 1413 (my accumulation: hole in step 1414); the kernel's `damagePlate` snaps a residue ≤ 1e-12 to 0, so the hole is in **1413** (23.55 s), and the scenario's own `ownRuinStep` uses the same snap, so the case is partly a test of the snap (same for the ×4 case: 1 − 3 × (1/3) = 1.1e-16). The decision is right physically (1413 exactly), the snap is in the code and a comment but not in §13l.1; #177 asks that a case on a threshold test that threshold, and this one does, but its criterion should say so. |
