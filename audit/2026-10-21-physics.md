# Physics audit: the models as one system (2026-10-21)

Independent physics auditor, read-only. Base: `origin/ccr-4f9cd066-gv15es`
at `05fba31`. Nothing in the code, the design or the decisions was changed;
every proposed resolution below is a proposal for the lead designer or the
owner (#180), not a decision.

Severity: **C** contradiction (two models, or a model and a law, rest on
incompatible assumptions); **U** undocumented assumption; **D** documented
simplification; **A** anchor error (a cited real magnitude is wrong, or the
game value is outside it); **Q** question.

Re-runnable numbers: every computed number below comes from
`node audit-calc.mjs` as reproduced in the appendix (it imports only `js/`),
unless the finding says otherwise.

## Question 1: one law or several

What a cell's resistance depends on in the current kernel:

| Channel | Resistance of a cell | Depends on mass? | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kinetic | r = c × σ, σ = cell mass / edge²; wrecked after `hitsToWreck` × r of sectional momentum | **yes** | `js/rooms.js:242-248`, `js/damage.js:112` |
| Round (the agent) | S = c × m / A₀ | yes (its own) | `js/rooms.js:225` |
| Beam, deposit | Bethe in σ | **yes** | `js/damage.js:186` |
| Beam, integrity | fixed `armour.burnEnergy` per cell | **no** | `js/rooms.js:281-284` |
| Blast | fixed `room.blastResistance` per cell × `hold.blastFactor` | **no** | `js/rooms.js:910-912` |
| Heat (temperature) | fixed `thermal.heatCapacityPerCell` per cell | **no** | `js/rooms.js:80` |
| Fragments / debris | no damage channel (debris flies; obstacles at L4, #9) | — | `js/rooms.js:1074-1081` |
| Shield vs round | fixed sectional momentum per round (from the S round) | no (round's mass ignored) | `js/field.js:38` |

So the kernel has **one law (mass on the path) for kinetic penetration and
beam deposit**, and **three per-cell constants** (burn, blast, heat
capacity) that ignore the masses #232/#234 introduced.

### F1 (C) Beam integrity ignores the mass the beam deposits into

- `DESIGN.md:1094` (§13i.1): "Cell i loses integrity at P·F_i /
  `armour.burnEnergy` per second (the energy that ruins **any cell** along
  the beam's channel ...)".
- `DECISIONS.md` #232: "Now **one law for rounds and beams**: a cell
  resists in proportion to its own mass over its face (σ)".
- `js/data.js:80`: `burnEnergy: P(3e7, ...)` "beam energy that ruins any
  cell along the beam's channel".

Computed (T1): energy to ruin a cell per kilogram of the cell: hold 10 000
J/kg, beam bay 5 455, engine 4 918, armour 3 000, crew 2 027, reactor
**240 J/kg**. The deposit follows σ (the reactor cell takes everything at
100 MeV) but the ruin does not, so heavy cells are the cheapest per kilogram
to burn. Unlike blast (F3), §13i.1 does not list this as "not in this pass":
I find no statement that it is a deliberate exception to #232.

Proposed: a beam strength per kilogram, Q_cell = q × cell mass, with q
calibrated on armour (30 MJ / 10 t = 3 000 J/kg), so L0's armour numbers
stay. Changes: a reactor cell needs 375 MJ (75 s of an M beam instead of
6 s), a hold cell 9 MJ.

### F2 (C) A destroyed cell ejects its whole mass whatever destroyed it

- `DESIGN.md:850` (§13f.1): a destroyed cell's "mass leaves the ship as
  **debris**".
- `js/data.js:80` note: "30 MJ melts about 25 kg of steel".
- `js/rooms.js:1074-1081` (`rebalance`): `drop = it.m - (now ? now.m : 0)`,
  the whole cell mass.

With F1 and F3, a 30 MJ beam or a 0.5 MJ blast share removes a whole
125 t reactor cell from the ship: ~5 000× the mass the beam melts. The
mass that leaves changes M, the centre of mass and I (the L3 laws). This
follows from F1/F3 and disappears if they are resolved.

Proposed: no change of its own; fix F1 and F3 so that what destroys a cell
scales with its mass.

### F3 (C, partly documented) Blast capacity is per cell, and the lightest cell absorbs the most

- `DESIGN.md:1095`: "Blast resistance stays a fixed energy per cell ...
  tying it to mass is a later question" (documented).
- `js/data.js:84` `room.blastResistance` 5e5 J, "half an armour cell";
  `js/data.js:204` `hold.blastFactor` 4.
- #232: "a cell resists in proportion to its own mass".

Computed (T1) J/kg of blast a cell absorbs: hold **667** (2 MJ on 3 t),
magazine 167, armour 100, beam bay 91, engine 82, crew 34, reactor **4**.
The documented part is "fixed per cell". The undocumented part is that the
hold, the lightest cell since #235, is now the strongest blast absorber
(2 MJ per cell, twice an armour cell's 1 MJ on 10 t), and "half an armour
cell" was chosen when every cell had the same mass. #18c's hold as a blast
buffer has a possible physical reading (free volume lowers an internal
blast's pressure), which is neither mass nor written down.

Proposed: decide which quantity blast acts on: mass (one constant J/kg, as
F1) or free volume (an empty hold's air volume against a full room's), and
write it into §13d.1; replace `hold.blastFactor` by that quantity, since a
bare factor is what #228 forbids.

### F4 (C) A round's hit gives the target ship no impulse

- `DESIGN.md:1088`: "(f = 1: the round is wrecked; **its remaining
  momentum goes to the ship** like a stopped round's)".
- #228: "an agent and what it meets exchange the same quantity (impulse for
  a round ...)".
- `js/rooms.js:242-253` (`fireRound`) returns `impulse` per cell and never
  applies it; no caller in `js/`, `proto/battle.js:273-280` or the scenarios
  applies it either (grep for `applyImpulse`/`fixtureImpulse` outside the
  recoil and fixture paths finds none).

The shooter side is built (recoil, `js/rooms.js:1104-1118`, measured in L3);
the target side is not. Computed: an M round brings 4 800 N·s, the recoil
is the same 4 800 N·s, so the target should turn by about as much as the
shooter (#236: 0.050 °/s per M shot on R1).

Proposed: `fireRound` applies Σ impulse (the momentum the ship's cells took)
at the hit cells to the body that holds them; momentum books then close
between the two ships.

### F5 (U) A round's kinetic energy disappears; a shield's and a beam's do not

- `DESIGN.md:109`: "Cut: kinetic energy ½mv² as damage" and #137.
- `DESIGN.md:116`: shields: "everything stopped, kinetic included, costs
  budget and **becomes heat**"; `js/field.js:49` `heat: energy`.
- Beam: "P·F_i is heat in cell i's room" (`DESIGN.md:1094`).
- `fireRound` (`js/rooms.js:242`) deposits no heat.

Cutting ½mv² *as damage* is documented; dropping it from the heat books is
not. The same 1.71 MJ that heats a shield generator when the field slows an
M round vanishes when a hull cell stops it. Computed: 1.92 MJ per M hit; one
turret at 0.5 /s puts 0.96 MW into the target, a fifth of an M beam's 5 MW.

Proposed: the energy lost by the round along its path is heat in the rooms
that took its momentum, in proportion to what each took (energy books close,
no constant). Effect on play small (≈0.8 K per hit in a 4-cell room).

### F6 (D) `kinetic.hitsToWreck` is mass-consistent but ignores the round's size

`js/data.js:56`; `DESIGN.md:790`. A passed cell loses 1 / 3 because it took
exactly its r, so the loss is (taken / r) / 3 for every cell: consistent
with mass. But integrity is lost per sectional momentum, not per momentum or
hole volume: an L round (A₀ 2.25× an S round's) passing a cell removes the
same 1/3 as an S round. Documented as the D pass's abstraction (#203 D);
noted because it is the one place where the round's area cancels out.

### F7 (D) Matter is iron-equivalent and steel-strong for every room

`DESIGN.md:1086-1087` ("iron-equivalent by mass"; "thin spaced plates
resist a hard round less per kilogram ... accepted by the owner"). Computed
with the kernel's own Bethe integrator, a 100 MeV proton's CSDA range is
7.709 g/cm² in water and 11.264 in iron: a crew room that is mostly water,
aluminium and air would stop the beam in about two thirds of the areal
density the kernel uses. Documented; recorded for the size of the effect.

### F8 (Q) Debris as a damage channel

#9 and `DESIGN.md:850` defer debris as obstacles to L4. There is no rule yet
for what debris (or a wrecked round's fragments) does to what it meets; by
#228 it would be the round rule with the debris's own σ. Question for L4.

## Question 2: mass, heat capacity and exponents

### F9 (C) Heat capacity is the old 1 t cell, against the masses #234 cites

- `js/data.js:113` `heatCapacityPerCell: P(6e5, ...)`; #202 (E): "L1's heat
  capacity already equals **1 t × 600 J/(kg·K)**".
- `DESIGN.md:966` (#221): "the heat capacity of the machinery, coolant and
  air coupled to the room's heat, not of its structure".
- `js/data.js:132` `cellMass.reactor` 1.25e5 kg, sourced from submarine
  reactor compartments (#234), i.e. the reactor's machinery, shielding and
  primary coolant, not hull structure.

Computed (T1): a cell's own mass × 450 J/(kg·K) (steel) against 6e5 J/K:
structure 2.3×, engine 4.6×, armour 7.5×, crew 11×, **reactor 94×**. The
reactor's cited 125 t is exactly the "machinery and coolant" #221 says C
stands for, yet C stays at the 1 t value. The beam makes it visible: it
deposits its energy in the cell's matter by σ (F1), and that energy then
heats a C that excludes that matter. Every heat race (reactor overheats
alone in 61 s, §13c.3) rests on this number.

Proposed: one rule, C per cell = Σ (thermally coupled mass × c_p) per room
type, sourced like the masses (or at least C ≥ the cell's machinery mass ×
c_p); the owner decides whether heat races may slow by 10–100× or whether
the remaining gap is a stylisation to be written down (§4 "borrow the shape,
tune the constant").

### F10 (C) The beam reaches deeper in cells at higher tiers; rounds do not

- `DESIGN.md:208`, #64: "Armour thickness scales with the cell, so small
  rounds cannot penetrate capital armour".
- `js/data.js:101` `beam.gradient` k = 0; E = gradient × N × edge (k = 1);
  σ k = 1; the Bethe range grows about as E^1.7.
- `DESIGN.md:1853` (§16): "at T6 a 400 MeV beam stays in the first cell (the
  square–cube law, #63, for L6)".

Computed (CSDA range / σ of an armour cell at the tier): the same bay N = 2:
T1 0.28 cell, T3 0.47, T6 **0.91**; N = 8: T1 2.91, T3 4.15, T6 **5.72**.
Kinetic depth is identical T1–T6 (P1). §16's sentence compares a T6 armour
column with 400 MeV, which at T6 is a bay of N ≈ 1.3, not the T1 bay of
N = 8 (that bay gives 2 400 MeV at T6); it is not the T6 equivalent of the
T1 case.

Proposed: decide the intended scaling. If a bay of N cells should reach the
same depth in cells at every tier (as rounds do), E must grow with the
tier so that range ∝ edge: roughly gradient k ≈ −0.4 (to be computed, not a
power law exactly). If deeper beams on big ships are intended, write it
into §5.4 and correct §16.

### F11 (U) Engine mass scales k = 3 while its thrust scales k = 2

`js/data.js:133` `cellMass.engine` (k = 3) "structure 3 t + a 15 MN engine
at the Saturn F-1's thrust per tonne"; `js/data.js:160` `engine.thrust`
(k = 2). Computed thrust per kilogram of engine machinery: T1 0.806 kN/kg
(the F-1's), T3 0.403, T6 **0.134**: the anchor holds only at T1. Same
pattern, smaller: `cellMass.beamBay` (k = 3, "1 t of accelerator per
metre") against `beam.power` (k = 2); `rcs.mass` (k = 3, `js/data.js:145`)
against `rcs.force` (k = 2), whereas `radiator.mass` correctly follows its
area (k = 2).

Proposed: split such masses into structure (k = 3) and machinery that
scales with what it delivers (k = 2), or document that machinery grows with
volume. Effect: big ships lighter; the square–cube law of #63 stays in the
structure.

### F12 (U) Notes still describe the 10 t cell

`js/data.js:143` (`rcs.force`): "a 2500 t corvette turns 90 degrees in
about 10 s"; `js/data.js:160` (`engine.thrust`): "a 2500 t corvette with two
reaches about 1.2 g". Both are the 10 t-per-cell ship of #221; the path
model's R1 is 850.1 t at 1.80 g (`DESIGN.md` §13j.1). `js/data.js:84`
`room.blastResistance` "half an armour cell" (from equal cell masses, see
F3); `cellMass.shieldGenerator`/`railgun` "the old average" (documented).

Proposed: update the notes to the current masses (text only).

### F13 (U) Repair restores integrity at one rate whatever the cell weighs

`js/data.js:178` `repair.rate` 0.02 /s (k = 0); `js/rooms.js:869`. Under
#232 a reactor cell's integrity is 125 t of machinery and a hold cell's 3 t;
both regain 1/3 in about 17 s. No document states that repair is per
integrity, not per kilogram.

Proposed: either document integrity as the unit of repair or make the
repair budget a mass rate (kg/s) so heavy cells repair slower.

### F14 (Q) Exponents that check out

Checked against what each quantity scales with: radiator area and mass
(k = 2, area); conduction per metre (k = 1: G ∝ wall area, air films or
coolant pipe section); power and ammunition per metre (k = 1; ammunition
gives rounds/s ∝ 1/edge, matching `turret.fireRate` k = −1); heat capacity,
charge, warhead, blast, explosion, magazine (k = 3); crew and life support
(k = 2, deck area); `matter.kineticResistance` (k = 0, keeps kinetic depth
tier-invariant); `shield.radius`, `repair.radius` (k = 1). `reactor.power`
k = 2 against reactor mass k = 3 is documented (§13c.3). Question only:
`armour.burnEnergy` k = 3 against beam power k = 2 makes burn times ∝ edge
(6 s T1 → 36 s T6), consistent with "heat races ∝ edge" (#195) but not
written for the beam.

## Question 3: the shield

Interaction points: (a) beam power at the hull entry point
(`js/rooms.js:797-820`, `fireBeam` takes the passed power); (b) rounds
(`js/rooms.js:839-856`, `js/field.js:38-50`); (c) power draw and routing
(`supply`); (d) heat of the generator (draw + absorbed + rounds' energy);
(e) EM brightness = absorbed (`js/rooms.js:495, 501`).

Symmetry check: a beam absorbed heats the generator by exactly the energy
absorbed (`js/rooms.js:495-498`: `s.paid * dt`), and a round slowed heats
it by exactly the kinetic energy removed (`js/field.js:49`). Those books
close. Two do not:

### F15 (C) The shield's kinetic stop and its buffer scale differently with tier

- `js/data.js:104` `shield.budget` k = 2; `:106` `kineticStop` k = 1 (one S
  round's sectional momentum); `:107` buffer = budget × 1 s.
- #171: an S round "is stopped at full strength by definition".

The stop is a sectional momentum (k = 1), so the velocity it leaves is the
same at every tier (800 → 266.7 m/s for an M round), but the energy that
costs grows with the round's mass (k = 3) while the buffer grows k = 2.
Computed, M rounds per full buffer at g = 1: T1 **5.86**, T3 2.93, T6
**0.98**. At T6 the buffer cannot fully slow a single M round. Not
documented anywhere I found.

Proposed: let the field's capacity be the quantity it pays (energy, k = 3
for impulses) or let the stop scale so the energy per round keeps pace with
the buffer; the lead designer chooses which of the two numbers is the law.

### F16 (C) Momentum the field removes goes nowhere

- #228 (impulse exchanged); §4 "Recoil (Newton 3)".
- `js/rooms.js:839-856` (`shieldRound`) lowers the round's velocity and
  books heat; no impulse is applied to the generator's ship.

Computed: an M round at g = 1 loses 3 200 N·s at T1 (691 200 N·s at T6),
two thirds of a recoil impulse, absent from the momentum books that L3
keeps exact.

Proposed: apply the removed momentum to the body holding the generator
(the field pushes on its source), at the generator's position.

### F17 (U) The shield reduces a beam's current, a round's speed

`fireBeam` (`js/rooms.js:271`) takes `power` after the shield and the
bay's `energy` unchanged: the shield removes particles and leaves the
survivors' energy, so the beam's depth is unchanged. For a round it lowers
velocity, hence depth (§13j.1: "stops in the first magazine cell" after the
shield). §13i.1 says only "The shield absorbs first, at the entry point";
which quantity it takes from a particle beam is not written.

Proposed: state it in §13i.1 (whichever is chosen); if the field acts on
each particle as it does on a round, it lowers E and the deposit moves
toward the surface.

### F18 (Q) Overlapping fields and rounds

`js/rooms.js:842` throws: "a round in overlapping fields: how the buffers
share it is a question for the designer". §13d.1 "Shield overlap" says "The
impulse buffer of each generator is shared the same way". The kernel and
the design disagree on whether the rule exists; prototype v2 and L4 will
need it.

### F19 (Q) Field strength from the generator's centre

`js/rooms.js:788` measures g(r) from the generator room's centre to the
entry point; the field falls with distance from the generator (#99), so a
generator placed at the hull protects that face nearly at full strength and
the far side at g(r) of the hull width. Consistent with #99; noted because
it interacts with facing (§13j.2) and nothing measures it yet.

## Question 4: time step and determinism

### F20 (U) A round is launched at the start of its step but stamped mid-step

`js/rooms.js:467` sets `s.lastShot = world.t + at` and the `shot` event at
`world.t + at`, but `recoil` (`js/rooms.js:1110`) pushes the round with
`t: world.t` at the room's position, and `shipStep` then moves it a full
dt. Error up to one step: 13.3 m along the line of fire at 800 m/s (2.7
cells at T1), 1/60 s of time of flight. Not noticeable as a miss (it is
along the line), but the round record and the event disagree.

Proposed: launch at `world.t + at` (move the round by (dt − at) v in its
first step).

### F21 (U) Heat deposited by the beam then partly leaves with the cell it destroys

`js/rooms.js:283-284`: every cell's heat is deposited, then damage is
applied; a cell destroyed in that step triggers `rebalance`, whose
`ventCell` (`js/rooms.js:1080`) removes that cell's share of the room's
heat, including part of the energy deposited in the same step. The other
order (damage, then deposit) gives a different heat. Deterministic, but an
ordering choice not written in §13i.1; P8 does not see it (its cells stand
alone, no ship).

### F22 (D) Step size: no outcome a player would notice found

- `fireRound` and `fireBeam` resolve a whole path at one instant, so the
  2.7 cells a round crosses per step do not matter in the kernel (the
  prototype's segment test is a STAND-IN, §13j.2).
- Semi-implicit Euler under 1.8 g for 1.88 s (time of flight at 1.5 km):
  31.58 m against 31.31 m exact, +0.28 m, under 1/16 of a cell
  (a dt² n(n+1)/2 against a (n dt)²/2, n = 113).
- Cell destruction by a beam lands on step ends; #236 (3) accepted it; the
  N = 2 case relies on the 1e-12 tolerance of `damageCell`
  (`js/rooms.js:175`): 1/60 s at most.
- Ordering: rooms run in insertion order (an array); Maps are keyed by room
  objects in insertion order; blast, power routing and fragments sort by id
  or by mass with a stable sort; every cluster flow uses start-of-step
  state. I found no order dependence other than F21. Floating sums depend
  on the order rooms were added (bit-level only, same inputs give the same
  result).
- Explicit Euler stability: dt·G/C = 2.8e-4 for two M coolant rooms over
  10 m, dt·4εσAT³/C = 3.5e-5 at 1000 K: far from 1 at every tier (both fall
  as 1/edge).

### F23 (A, minor) The F-1 anchor uses sea-level thrust in space

`js/data.js:133`: "a 15 MN engine at the Saturn F-1's thrust per tonne (8.4
t for 6.8 MN)". 6.77 MN is the F-1's sea-level thrust; in vacuum it gives
about 7.77 MN. Computed: 0.806 against 0.925 kN/kg; the engine machinery
per cell would be 2.7 t, not 3.1 t (6.1 → 5.7 t per cell, inside the range).

### F24 (A, question) The reactor's "low end" is below the cited range

`js/data.js:132`: "1000 kg/m³: US submarine reactor compartments, 1130-1680
t in about 10 m x 12 m, low end". Read as a cylinder 10 m in diameter and
12 m long (942 m³), the cited low end is 1130 / 942 ≈ **1200 kg/m³**, not
1000. Read otherwise, the volume is not stated. The game value may be
below its own cited range by about 17 %. I could not re-check the source
masses (no network to Hanford/GlobalSecurity in this sandbox).

Proposed: state the volume used, or raise to the computed low end.

### F25 (U) Constants with neither a source nor an "estimate" label

From `js/data.js`: `thermal.heatCapacityPerCell` (6e5, "sets the pace",
see F9), `armour.blastResistance` and `room.blastResistance`,
`hold.blastFactor`, `kinetic.hitsToWreck`, `conduction.wall`,
`lifeSupport.capacityPerCell`, `turret.heatPerShot`, `reactor.power` and
`reactor.explosionEnergy`, `engine.powerDraw`, `rcs.force`,
`beambay.efficiency`, `repair.rate`. They cite decision numbers only. Some
are game constants by nature (hits to wreck, repair); the ones that claim
physics (heat capacity, blast resistance, explosion energy) would benefit
from a source or the word "estimate". `structure.cellMass`, `cellMass.engine`
and `cellMass.beamBay` are correctly labelled estimates.

### F26 (Q) `beambay.efficiency` 0.4 was set for a maser

`js/data.js:157` (#183); #202 listed it as "laser-like efficiency 40 %".
Since #229 the weapon is a neutral particle beam: RF to beam, then a
neutraliser (stripping or attachment, typically well under 100 %). The
note was not revisited; a real wall-plug efficiency for an accelerator plus
neutraliser is lower than 0.4 in my understanding, but I did not find a
source to cite and I do not give a number.

## Question 6: gates

### F27 (D) Mechanics still waiting for their measured effect

### F28 (Q) HE contact bursts have no kernel path

`he.coupling` (`js/data.js:94`) and `armour.blastResistance`
(`js/data.js:79`) are read only by the pure `blast()` of `js/damage.js:42`
